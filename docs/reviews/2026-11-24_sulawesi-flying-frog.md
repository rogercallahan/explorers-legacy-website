## What the new article states

A new Science article, added 24 Nov. 2026, says scientists have named a flying frog, *Rhacophorus kurniatiae*, from the mountain rainforests of Sulawesi, Indonesia.

- Zootaxa 5900(4), published 11 Nov. 2026, by Trilaksono, Racho, Engilis, Hitch, and Herlambang, is the peer-reviewed description. The lesson uses it for the species, the jelly egg masses on plants above or near water, the statement that this way of laying eggs had not been recorded before among Sulawesi’s flying frogs, the close relative *Rhacophorus boeadii* and the small DNA difference, and the suggestion about seasonal ponds.
- UC Davis News, Tiffany Dobbyn, 17 Nov. 2026, is the news report. The lesson uses it for the bright green frogs at Danau Coca-Cola in the Mekongga Mountains, more than 6,100 feet above sea level, Alan Hitch’s words, gliding on webbed feet, the 2009 to 2013 measurements and calls, the name for Hellen Kurniati, and the note that a national park has not happened yet.
- The article keeps a verified list separate from what is still uncertain. Why the frog uses jelly instead of foam still needs testing. The paper describes the frog from this one high lake. How many live there is not known. More species from these expeditions are still being described.
- No photograph is on the page. The photographs available for this frog are copyrighted, so the article has no image.

## Learner notes

The article explains gliding and the evidence for a new species in everyday language. The away-from-the-screen activity asks a trusted adult to compare clear hair gel with soapy foam, then draw the result in a paper journal. The article says this is only a model, so it cannot prove what the frogs do, and it does not ask anyone to post the journal. It is a starting explanation. It has not been field-tested with children, and it is not a validated assessment.
