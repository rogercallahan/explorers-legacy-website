## What was already here

Engineering already had a short passage, “A coming milestone: one light-day from Earth,” inside “Reaching beyond the Sun’s bubble.” It gave NASA’s predicted clock time and distance, and it said the moment was still ahead as of the October 8 preview. That passage is now a pointer to a new article. The prediction is no longer presented as still in the future.

## What the new article states

A new Engineering article, added 19 Nov. 2026, says Voyager 1 reached one light-day on Wednesday, November 18, 2026.

- NASA’s November 19 news release states 2:16 a.m. Pacific Standard Time (5:16 a.m. Eastern Standard Time), about 16.1 billion miles (25.9 billion kilometers), and a radio signal of about 24 hours one way.
- NASA’s distance tracker states 16.094 billion miles (25.902 billion kilometers) at 2:16:07 a.m. Pacific Standard Time. That converts to 4:16:07 a.m. Central Standard Time. The article says “about 4:16 a.m. Central Time” for that conversion. The tracker and the “What Is a Light-Day” page, when read on 19 Nov. 2026, still described the moment as upcoming. The news release is the page that states the milestone was reached.
- “What Is a Light-Day” gives the exact distance 16,094,799,096 miles (25,902,068,356 kilometers), explains that a light-day is distance, and dates the launch to Sept. 5, 1977.
- Science News, Mandana Tadayon, published 18 Nov. 2026, is cited as an independent report. The measurements follow the NASA pages.
- The article says science and engineering data had not arrived since November 14, and that engineers confirmed the distance with a carrier signal and path calculations. That open engineering question stays separate from the distance.
