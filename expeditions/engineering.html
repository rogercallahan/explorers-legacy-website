ENG-001Paper Bridge

Family activity; adapt together · About 20–40 minutes; pause whenever useful

Begin with a question

Which fold helps paper span a gap?

Gather

Two books, paper, ruler, large identical blocks.

Before you begin: Build on the floor with lightweight blocks; never stand on a model.

Predict, explore, revise

  1. Set books ten centimeters apart and bridge them with a flat sheet.
  2. Add blocks one at a time until the paper sags; stop before anything falls.
  3. Repeat using an accordion-folded sheet with the same span and paper size.

Keep your evidence

Two designs and their supported block counts.

Make sense of it

Shape changes how a structure carries a load; a fair comparison keeps material and span similar.

Keep wondering

Can you improve the bridge with the same sheet?

Share your reasoning: Explain what you observed, what you think it means, and one thing this activity cannot establish. A surprising result or an honest “I don’t know yet” is useful evidence.

Make it work for you: Sketch, dictate to a trusted adult, or describe your evidence instead of writing. A partner can handle materials or measurements. If a step is inaccessible, discuss its prediction and clearly label it untested.
