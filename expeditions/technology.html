TEC-001Signals in the Sky

Family activity; adapt together · About 20–40 minutes; pause whenever useful

Begin with a question

What can an aircraft observation tell us, and what remains uncertain?

Open the original Signals in the Sky expedition

Gather

Notebook, safe sky view; optional adult-operated flight map.

Before you begin: Never observe while driving, enter restricted areas or point lights at aircraft. Adults handle online maps; keep exact home coordinates private.

Predict, explore, revise

  1. Open the existing ELS Signals in the Sky field notes linked below.
  2. From a safe place, record the time and describe one aircraft or record that none was visible.
  3. Compare direct observations with any map information and label each source.

Keep your evidence

An observation, its source and an explanation of a mismatch or missing information.

Make sense of it

A digital map and a direct sighting provide different evidence; neither should be treated as a complete picture.

Keep wondering

What could explain an aircraft missing from a map?

Share your reasoning: Explain what you observed, what you think it means, and one thing this activity cannot establish. A surprising result or an honest “I don’t know yet” is useful evidence.

Make it work for you: Sketch, dictate to a trusted adult, or describe your evidence instead of writing. A partner can handle materials or measurements. If a step is inaccessible, discuss its prediction and clearly label it untested.
