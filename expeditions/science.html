SCI-001Shadow Detectives

Family activity; adapt together · Three brief observations across one day

Begin with a question

How does a shadow change during a day?

Gather

Paper, pencil, ruler, a fixed outdoor object visible from a safe spot.

Before you begin: Never look at the Sun or use optical equipment to view it. Stay on safe ground with an adult.

Predict, explore, revise

  1. Sketch the object's shadow and record the time without looking at the Sun.
  2. Return to the same spot twice later and measure its shadow.
  3. Compare lengths and directions while keeping the object fixed.

Keep your evidence

A table of three times, lengths and sketches.

Make sense of it

Earth's rotation changes the Sun's apparent position, so a fixed object's shadow changes. Clouds can make its edge uncertain.

Keep wondering

How would the results differ on another day?

Share your reasoning: Explain what you observed, what you think it means, and one thing this activity cannot establish. A surprising result or an honest “I don’t know yet” is useful evidence.

Make it work for you: Sketch, dictate to a trusted adult, or describe your evidence instead of writing. A partner can handle materials or measurements. If a step is inaccessible, discuss its prediction and clearly label it untested.

SCI-002Moon Journal

Family activity; adapt together · Five evenings; about 10 minutes each

Begin with a question

Does the Moon look the same every evening?

Gather

Notebook, pencil, safe window or outdoor view.

Before you begin: Observe from a safe place with an adult; do not walk in the dark while looking upward.

Predict, explore, revise

  1. Record the date, time and a sketch of the Moon when visible.
  2. Repeat on five evenings; write not visible when clouds or timing prevent a sighting.
  3. Compare the lit shapes without inventing missing observations.

Keep your evidence

Five dated entries including gaps.

Make sense of it

The Moon's phases reflect changing views of its sunlit half. A cloudy night is missing evidence, not a new phase.

Keep wondering

What would a longer observation period reveal?

SCI-019Pebble Shadow Map

Family activity; adapt together · About 20–40 minutes; pause whenever useful

Begin with a question

How can lighting imitate changes in terrain?

Gather

Stone, LED flashlight, paper.

Before you begin: Use a clean stone and LED flashlight; avoid eyes.

Predict, explore, revise

  1. Draw the stone's surface under light from the left.
  2. Keep the stone still and move the light to the right.
  3. Mark which apparent features changed and which remained identifiable.

Keep your evidence

Two sketches with light direction arrows.

Make sense of it

Shadows can hide or emphasize surface features. Comparing images requires attention to illumination.

What living things use a small patch of ground?

Gather

Window view, notebook, timer.

Predict, explore, revise

Three dated tallies and one uncertainty.

Observation effort and time of day affect what is detected. Not seeing an organism does not prove absence.

What separates when a soil-water mixture rests?

Gather

Clear plastic container with lid, garden soil, water, spoon.

Particles can settle at different rates. Layers alone do not identify every mineral or prove the water is safe.
