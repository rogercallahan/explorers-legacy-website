ART-001Observation Sketchbook

Family activity; adapt together · About 20–40 minutes; pause whenever useful

Begin with a question

What changes when we draw from observation instead of memory?

Gather

Paper, pencil, ordinary household object.

Before you begin: Choose a safe object and work in comfortable lighting.

Predict, explore, revise

  1. Draw the object from memory for two minutes.
  2. Place it in view and make a second drawing with labels for details.
  3. Compare what you noticed rather than judging which drawing is prettier.

Keep your evidence

Two drawings and three newly noticed details.

Make sense of it

Drawing can make attention visible; observation and interpretation both influence an image.

Keep wondering

Which detail would you investigate more closely?

Share your reasoning: Explain what you observed, what you think it means, and one thing this activity cannot establish. A surprising result or an honest “I don’t know yet” is useful evidence.

Make it work for you: Sketch, dictate to a trusted adult, or describe your evidence instead of writing. A partner can handle materials or measurements. If a step is inaccessible, discuss its prediction and clearly label it untested.
